# Demo content for the three showcase decks (fiction; every style uses this text word for word)

Reader: Sam, an analyst at a small software company, the evening after showing a customer what drives renewals. Everyone is invented: Morgan (the customer's head of operations), Jo (their admin lead), Kit (Sam's manager). Small "made-up example" label in a corner of the first slide.

## Hero
You did a hard thing today.
Your first renewals analysis, shown live to the customer. And they want to use it.

## What Morgan actually said (her own words from the notes)
- "So it's really about getting a second person in?"   (she put your main finding in her own words)
- "This is going straight into our plan for next quarter."
- "Can we try it on the new sign-ups first?"
- "Can I show this to my boss on Monday?"

## What the pause really means
Morgan's team has a product launch this month, so new analysis waits until after it. It is a scheduling pause, not a "no".

## What went well
1. Morgan put your finding in her own words and wants it in next quarter's plan.
2. Jo already knew which accounts have only one user: he is already working on it.
3. Your three options gave them something to choose from.

## Presenting: five things to try next time
1. Lead with the answer. Open with three sentences: the finding, who it applies to, the choice. Evidence after. (Morgan asked "So which one first?" 15 minutes before the options slide.)
2. Leave out any slide you would introduce with a doubt. Send it later instead.
3. Skip the apology at the start. Kit had already said it was a first look.
4. Email Jo your process questions before the call, so the slide says the answer.
5. End with one question that asks for a choice: "Which of these fits your launch plans best?"

## Workflow: four changes (most of these are on the AI's side)
1. Freeze the content the day before. Changes after that go into the version you email.
2. Make a call version of 8 to 10 pages, separate from the full deck.
3. No new features on call day. A rehearsal beats a pause button.
4. Before the next phase, agree with the customer what a good result would look like.

## Time
Keep the last two to three hours before a call for one timed run-through out loud, and lunch. Tell Claude a stop time and it will stop.
